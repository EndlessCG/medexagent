MedExAgent: Training LLM Agents to Ask, Examine, and Diagnose in Noisy Clinical Environments

Yicheng Gao1, Xiaolin Zhou2, Yahan Li1, Yue Zhao1, Ruishan Liu1

TL;DR

We model interactive diagnosis as a POMDP over questioning, exam ordering, and diagnosis with noisy observations and exam costs, then train an 8B MedExAgent via SFT+DAPO to match 70B baselines on OOD diagnostic accuracy.

Disclaimer

MedExAgent is a research prototype. It must not be used for medical advice or patient care.

Environment and training

One episode mixes three kinds of action: the agent asks the patient questions, orders exams as tool calls, and ends the episode with a diagnosis. We formalize this as a POMDP in which noise enters through the observation function. MedExAgent starts from Meditron3-8B. It is first fine-tuned on synthetic doctor–patient conversations that follow the five-stage Calgary–Cambridge interview model, then trained with DAPO in the environment on a composite reward R = Rdx + 0.5·Rtool − 0.1·Rcost, which scores diagnostic accuracy, tool-call quality, and the financial cost and patient discomfort of unnecessary exams. Training uses DDxPlus and PMC-Patients-v2; AgentClinic-MedQA is held out as an out-of-distribution test set.

Noise model

Patient responses are perturbed with probability pconv = 0.3 and exam results with pexam = 0.1, using seven patient noise types and three exam noise types drawn from error classifications in medical research. Noise is injected during both SFT data generation and RL rollouts.

Main results

As an in-distribution sanity check, MedExAgent-8B significantly outperforms every baseline on DDxPlus and PMC-Patients-v2 (p < 0.001 on all metrics). On the held-out AgentClinic-MedQA benchmark, MedExAgent-8B is statistically tied with the 70B Aloe-Beta on Sim, Jac, and Acc.

It also ties Qwen3-32B on strict accuracy (p = 0.857) and outperforms the remaining five baselines on Jac. At 8B parameters, MedExAgent matches the strongest medical specialist in the comparison at roughly 9× smaller parameter count. Significance is tested with a paired percentile bootstrap (B = 10,000) on the same split.

Exam cost

The reward charges each unnecessary exam for its financial cost and patient discomfort. On PMC-Patients-v2, MedExAgent orders 4.03 exams per case, close to the 3.46 exams in the recorded ground-truth workup.

Abstract

Real-world clinical diagnosis is a complex process in which the doctor is required to obtain information from both interaction with the patient and conducting medical exams. Additionally, the doctor needs to adapt to different patient personas, as well as noisy and incomplete information that can happen at any time during the process. However, existing benchmarks for medical LLMs and methods for automatic diagnosis largely simplify this process by reducing it to single-turn question answering, noise-free conversations, or sequential exam making, etc., ignoring the interactive and uncertain nature of clinical diagnosis. In this paper, we aim to address this gap by formalizing clinical diagnosis as a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP) with three action types: questioning the patient, ordering medical exams as tool calls, and issuing a diagnosis. We also introduce a systematic noise model comprising seven patient noise types and three exam noise types.

Using our proposed environment, we train an effective diagnosis agent, MedExAgent, through a two-stage pipeline that first performs supervised finetuning on synthetic conversations structured after the Calgary-Cambridge model for clinical interviews, and then applies DAPO to optimize a composite reward capturing diagnostic accuracy, tool call quality, and exam cost including financial cost and patient discomfort. Through extensive experiments and ablation studies, we demonstrate that MedExAgent achieves diagnostic performance comparable to larger models while maintaining cost-efficient examination strategies.
